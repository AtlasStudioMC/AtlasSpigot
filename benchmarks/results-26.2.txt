AtlasSpigot 26.2 (build 21) vs Paper 26.2 (build 121) - MSPT under entity load
==============================================================================

VERDICT: INCONCLUSIVE. AtlasSpigot measured faster on average, but the run-to-run
noise on this hardware is larger than the difference, so no number is claimed from
this data. Details below.

Test environment
  Hardware      Apple M3 Pro laptop, 18GB RAM   <- see "why this is inconclusive"
  JVM           OpenJDK 25.0.2, -Xms4G -Xmx4G, Aikar's flags (identical both sides)
  Seed          2618050634530417871
  Plugin        benchmarks/atlas-bench, 300 entities, fixed spawn seed 12345
  Window        140 tick warmup, then 400 consecutive ticks sampled via ServerTickEndEvent
  Paper build   paper-26.2-121, sha256 0de30efb024bc8b83c9c7d507d11802897ad8056b6110ec09fe1a91d126ccb54
  Atlas build   atlasspigot-26.2-21 (7 optimisation patches + maximum tuning config)
  Design        8 runs total, alternating P,A,P,A,... so drift hits both sides equally
  Configs       AtlasSpigot ran this project's shipped tuned config; Paper ran its own
                untouched defaults. So this compares the products as shipped, not the
                jars in isolation.

Raw results (avg MSPT, lower is better)
  Paper   10.1848   8.8930  15.5838  17.9500     mean 13.15  sd 3.74
  Atlas    5.0319  13.0448  14.5008  11.1931     mean 10.94  sd 3.61

  Paired by run order:
    pair 1   P 10.18   A  5.03    +5.15  (Atlas faster)
    pair 2   P  8.89   A 13.04    -4.15  (Paper faster)
    pair 3   P 15.58   A 14.50    +1.08  (Atlas faster)
    pair 4   P 17.95   A 11.19    +6.76  (Atlas faster)

  Mean difference  +2.21 MSPT, i.e. 16.8% in AtlasSpigot's favour
  Pooled sd         3.67 MSPT
  Effect / noise    0.60

Why this is inconclusive
  The effect-to-noise ratio is 0.60. A result only starts to mean something when that
  clearly exceeds 1, and the two ranges overlap heavily (Paper 8.89-17.95, Atlas
  5.03-14.50). One of four pairs favours Paper outright.

  The dominant signal is not the software, it is thermal throttling. Over the session
  Paper degraded 10.18 -> 17.95 (+76%) and AtlasSpigot 5.03 -> 11.19 (+122%) purely as a
  function of run order. A fanless-class laptop cannot hold a steady clock through eight
  consecutive 4GB Minecraft servers, and no amount of alternating fixes that - it only
  stops the drift from being mistaken for a win by one side.

  An earlier batch of three runs, before this one, produced the opposite ordering
  (Paper 6.99 / 8.59, Atlas 8.62). Which side "wins" depends on which runs you look at.
  That is the clearest possible sign the measurement cannot resolve the difference.

What would fix it
  Dedicated hardware with a stable clock - a desktop or a bare-metal server, not a
  laptop - and more runs per side. Until then, AtlasSpigot's 26.2 performance is
  genuinely unmeasured, and nothing on the website claims otherwise.
